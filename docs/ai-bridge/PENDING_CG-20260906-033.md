### PROBLEM
Current `main` contains two incompatible zoom contracts. `engine-h.js` deliberately computes `pixelPerfectZoom(target)` so `(zoom * activeDpr)` is an integer physical scale, marks the mode `hidpi-pixel-perfect-v2`, sets `CONFIG.roundPixels=true`, and globally disables image smoothing. But `engine-z.js` loads later and overwrites `CONFIG.zoom` with a viewport-fit fractional value `screenW/(targetTiles*32)` clamped to 1.05..1.45. Therefore the final LIVE zoom can be non-integer in physical pixels even though the earlier HD contract reports pixel-perfect intent.

For the current audit viewports this is material: 390 CSS px mobile gives `390/(11*32)=1.1079545...`; with the mobile DPR cap of 2, the physical world scale is about 2.2159 rather than an integer. 1280 CSS px desktop computes above the cap and becomes zoom 1.45; at DPR1 the physical scale is 1.45. `engine-ab.js` and the generic appearance renderer also round destination sprite positions/sizes while `engine-c.js` then applies the fractional world transform. This combination can preserve hard nearest-neighbor edges while still producing uneven pixel widths, resampling phase changes and temporal shimmer/crawl during camera/player motion. Increasing the avatar from 1.15x to 1.20x changes the destination sampling dimensions again, so scale quality cannot be judged only from a static screenshot.

There is a second sampling concern: `hero.PNG` frames are 256x384 and the live hero crops roughly 5% X/4% Y, then draws that large source region down to roughly 55x93 CSS px at 1.15x with `imageSmoothingEnabled=false`. Nearest-neighbor is explicitly appropriate for preserving sharp pixel-art edges when enlarging, but this hero path is a strong downscale followed by another world zoom. A 1.20x change will alter which source pixels survive nearest-neighbor downsampling. We need to measure whether the desired larger avatar improves readability or instead creates aliasing/shimmer/detail instability.

### EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE
- MDN documents that `CanvasRenderingContext2D.imageSmoothingEnabled=false` prevents smoothing when images are scaled and is useful for pixel-art rendering, especially enlargement. It guarantees sharp/unsmoothed resampling, not that arbitrary fractional scales map every source pixel to an equal number of destination pixels: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/CanvasRenderingContext2D/imageSmoothingEnabled
- MDN documents that `CanvasRenderingContext2D.scale()` changes how canvas units map to pixels; e.g. 0.5 makes one unit half a pixel and 2 makes one unit two pixels. Therefore a world scale such as 1.45 or 1.10795 inherently maps logical units fractionally before DPR: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/CanvasRenderingContext2D/scale
- W3C CSSWG issue #5837 explicitly discusses distortion from nearest-neighbor/pixelated rendering at non-integer scale factors such as 150%; integer multiples are the clean case while fractional factors can create unequal pixel sizes. This is strong counterevidence to assuming `image-rendering:pixelated` alone makes arbitrary zooms pixel-perfect: https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/5837
- Community reports from Godot/pixel-art projects consistently describe shimmer/jitter or uneven pixels under fractional camera/viewport scaling even with nearest filtering, and note that simply rounding every gameplay position can introduce a different class of motion jitter. This is evidence to benchmark final screen-space output rather than blindly snap physics: https://www.reddit.com/r/godot/comments/1php1x7/ and https://www.reddit.com/r/godot/comments/1ti0wws/
- Counterevidence: strict integer-only world zoom is not automatically the best visual result for Kelo World. The hero source is high-resolution relative to its final 55x93 CSS presentation and the project may prefer smoother composition/viewport coverage over mathematically square source pixels. The correct choice must come from same-trace captures and temporal metrics, not pixel-art dogma.

### HYPOTHESIS
Part of any apparent lateral sprite shimmer, crawling edges, uneven limb thickness or 'cheap' motion may come from the render sampling pipeline rather than the four animation frames themselves: the hero is rounded in world space, globally transformed by a fractional camera zoom, and sampled nearest-neighbor from a much larger source. Scaling the avatar to 1.20x could either help by giving important details more destination pixels or worsen instability by changing nearest-neighbor sample selection and making fractional-scale artifacts more visible.

A second hypothesis is that the correct Kelo solution may be hybrid rather than globally integer-only: preserve smooth float physics/camera, but benchmark a presentation-space strategy that stabilizes the final actor raster independently from world collision. Possible candidates include DPR-aware screen-space snapping of actor destination origin only, a pre-resampled/cached hero frame at selected presentation sizes, or quantized zoom steps that stay closer to integer physical scale. None should be implemented until the baseline proves which component causes visible instability.

### DO_NOT_ASSUME
- Do not assume `imageSmoothingEnabled=false` means the final output is pixel-perfect at fractional zoom.
- Do not assume `engine-h` is the final zoom owner; `engine-z` currently loads later and overwrites it.
- Do not round or snap physics/collider positions to solve a raster problem.
- Do not quantize the camera globally without measuring lateral smoothness; prior research already identified camera behavior as a separate variable.
- Do not enable smoothing globally; world atlases/pixel-art assets may require nearest filtering.
- Do not assume 1.20x is worse or better. It must be measured at motion, not just judged in a static frame.
- Do not add WebGL/WebGPU for this issue before Canvas2D baseline proves a bottleneck or sampling limitation that cannot be solved within the current renderer.

### RISKS
- Integer-only zoom may break the desired amount of world visible on devices with awkward viewport sizes; composition can matter more than perfect pixel squares.
- Rounding the actor in world coordinates already occurs; adding another indiscriminate snap can create double quantization and visible stutter.
- Pre-resampling can improve stability but increases cached texture memory and preprocessing complexity; doing it per frame/per scale at runtime would be unacceptable if it allocates frequently.
- Filtered downsampling could blur deliberately hard pixel art, while nearest downsampling can discard high-frequency source detail. Asset intent must be established from visual evidence.
- 1.20x may interact with the previously identified bounds/nameplate/occlusion contract; this experiment must keep those measurements active even though it focuses on sampling quality.
- A static screenshot can miss the failure entirely. Temporal capture is required.
