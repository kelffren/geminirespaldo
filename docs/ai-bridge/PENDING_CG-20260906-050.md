### PROBLEM
Kelo's live avatar presentation already exposes `shadowAnchorX` / `shadowAnchorY`, both equal to the foot root, but `engine-ab.js::renderAvatar()` only draws the hero sprite and nameplate. It does not render a neutral contact/blob shadow at that anchor. A later wrapper, `src/systems/armor-aura.js`, draws aura rings/glow/particles, but those are progression effects rather than a universal grounding shadow and their ring center is derived from `p.x` and `p.y + physicsRadius * 0.72`, not from `shadowAnchorY`.

This matters before adding lateral lean, bob, squash or a 1.20x avatar because those presentation effects intentionally move or enlarge the body while the perceived ground contact should remain stable. Without an independent low-frequency grounding cue, the body can look as if it floats or skates even when `footRoot` is mathematically stable.

### CONFIRMED_IN_GEMINI
- `engine-ab.js` current live presentation contract (`foot-root-scale-v2`) computes:
  - `footRootX = p.x`
  - `footRootY = p.y + 10`
  - `shadowAnchorX = footRootX`
  - `shadowAnchorY = footRootY`
- The same live renderer draws the sprite with `ctx.drawImage(...)`, then the nameplate. No shadow is drawn in that override.
- `src/systems/armor-aura.js` wraps the final `renderAvatar` and calls `renderBack() -> base() -> renderFront()`.
- Aura `drawRing()` draws ellipses around `p.x, p.y + physicsRadius * 0.72`; it calls `visualMetrics()` but does not consume `shadowAnchorX/Y`.
- Aura may be rank 0 and therefore render nothing at all; consequently it cannot be treated as the baseline contact-shadow contract.
- `scripts/live-hero-audit.mjs` validates presentation, collider, footRoot, enlarged visual bounds, aura scaling, gait/reversal/release, but contains no measurement for a shadow, shadow anchor screen position, or body-to-shadow separation.
- `engine-c.js` renders actors between `drawPreActors()` and `drawPostActors()` under the world camera transform, which gives a natural location for an actor-local ground cue before the sprite if it is implemented inside the avatar path or an explicit below-actor pass.
- Relevant ownership is duplicated rather than centralized: `engine-ab.js` owns live hero presentation/draw, `armor-aura.js` wraps the avatar for progression VFX, and `engine-c.js` orchestrates actor/world layers. Do not refactor these blindly before baseline/change/same-trace measurement.

### EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE
Official / engine documentation:
- Unity URP documents `Shadow Caster 2D` as a separate 2D shadow-casting concern with an explicit shape/silhouette contract. This supports keeping ground shadow semantics separate from the character sprite's animation state rather than baking grounding into arbitrary frames. Source: Unity 6 Manual, "Create shadows with Shadow Caster 2D in URP".
- Godot's 2D lights/shadows documentation likewise separates sprites, lights and shadow occluders. This is evidence for a distinct shadow layer/representation, not proof that Kelo needs dynamic light-cast shadows.

Community / practical evidence:
- A Godot 2D discussion recommends treating the character shadow as a separate object and placing it at the bottom/ground contact of the player while grounded, changing it only when height from the floor changes. This matches Kelo's existing foot-root/shadow-anchor separation.
- A gamedev discussion about pixel-art character grounding explicitly recommends bottom/pivot alignment and notes that contact/ground shadows help attach the sprite to the world.
- HD-2D community discussion also shows a counterexample: full silhouette projected shadows can reveal the flatness of a sprite and become visually distracting. Therefore the first Kelo candidate should be a restrained contact/blob shadow, not a complex directional silhouette shadow.

### HYPOTHESIS
A small neutral contact shadow anchored strictly to `shadowAnchorX/Y` can improve perceived weight and foot planting during lateral movement, reversals and future presentation-only lean/bob without touching physics. The benefit should be strongest when the avatar body moves visually relative to the foot root.

The shadow should not follow body bob vertically. It should follow world ground position, and its size/opacity may react only subtly to gait/support state if measurement shows value.

### PROPOSED_CHANGE
Do not change production yet.

Candidate A — baseline:
- Current live hero; no neutral contact shadow.

Candidate B — QA-only static contact shadow:
- Draw a low-alpha ellipse immediately before the hero sprite.
- Center = `layout.shadowAnchorX`, `layout.shadowAnchorY` plus at most a tiny authored Y correction measured against visible shoe contact.
- Size derived from `layout.visualWidth`, not `p.radius`, so 1.15x/1.20x scale can be compared deliberately.
- Suggested experimental range only, not approved values:
  - `rx = visualWidth * 0.28 .. 0.38`
  - `ry = rx * 0.22 .. 0.32`
  - alpha roughly `0.10 .. 0.20`
- No blur-heavy filter in the first pass; keep Canvas2D cost deterministic and style-compatible.
- The shadow remains unchanged by LEFT mirror.

Candidate C — only after B wins:
- Very small support-state modulation (for example width/alpha variation synchronized to planted/support phase), still ground anchored.
- Must not visibly pulse every frame or become a second animation competing with the feet.

Do not use `armor-aura` ring geometry as the contact shadow. Aura is rank-dependent, semantic progression VFX and has a different anchor contract.

### DO_NOT_ASSUME
- Do not assume the lack of a blob shadow is currently a visible defect until A/B screenshots and temporal traces are compared.
- Do not assume a more realistic/directional shadow is automatically better; full silhouette shadows can expose sprite flatness and create lighting inconsistencies.
- Do not move collider, physics root, foot root, depth root or camera to accommodate the shadow.
- Do not couple shadow position to future visual bob/lean; the purpose is to provide an independent ground reference.
- Do not refactor `renderAvatar`/aura/world layer ownership as part of the first experiment.
- Do not claim 1.20x is safe solely because shadow scales with `visualWidth`; nameplate, occlusion, raster and support-foot benchmarks remain separate gates.

- `shadowMirrorJumpPx` at RIGHT<->LEFT reversal — target 0.
- `shadowScaleRatio115vs120` — predictable and documented.

Human visual decision:
- Does Candidate B make the avatar look more planted without looking like a dark sticker or dirty halo?
- Does it reduce perceived skating especially during reversal and future body-only lean/bob?
- Does it remain subtle enough under authored environment lighting?

### RISKS
- Too large/dark a blob can make the sprite look pasted onto the ground.
- A shadow inconsistent with scene light direction can reduce HD-2D coherence; keep the first candidate intentionally contact-like and neutral.
- Scaling shadow directly with visualWidth may overgrow it at 1.20x; benchmark rather than assume linear scaling is best.
- If many actors receive the shadow, translucent ellipse overdraw can accumulate on low-end mobile; benchmark actor counts.
- Drawing the shadow in the wrong layer can cause props/foreground to occlude it incorrectly or make it appear above ground objects.
- Aura ring and contact shadow can visually interfere at higher aura ranks; test rank 0 and representative high ranks separately.
