### PROBLEM
The current production `hero.PNG` renderer replaces the previous procedural `renderAvatar()` implementation after the sprite loads successfully. The earlier renderer in `engine-d.js` drew a translucent elliptical ground shadow at `p.y + 14` before drawing the body. The live sprite renderer in `engine-ab.js` does not draw a ground shadow and, once `ok && sheet`, does not call the captured `_av` renderer; `_av` is only a load/failure fallback. The later `armor-aura.js` wrapper adds aura rings/glow/particles but no ground-contact shadow. Therefore the production hero can lose an inexpensive depth/grounding cue exactly while the current user priority is to make lateral movement feel more planted and to enlarge the avatar without making it appear to float.

### CONFIRMED_IN_GEMINI
- `ENGINE_MAP.md` identifies `engine-ab.js + assets/hero.PNG` as the OWNER LIVE hero renderer and `engine-ac.js` as gait owner; earlier renderers are legacy.
- `index.html` currently declares Kelo World V6.23 and loads `engine-d.js` before `engine-ab.js`, then later loads `armor-aura.js`.
- `engine-d.js` defines a procedural `renderAvatar()` whose first body-related draw is a ground ellipse: `ctx.ellipse(p.x, p.y + 14, p.radius * 0.9, p.radius * 0.45, ...)` with `rgba(0,0,0,0.4)`.
- `engine-ab.js` captures the prior renderer as `const _av = renderAvatar`, but when the production sprite is ready it draws only `hero.PNG` plus the nameplate. It returns to `_av` only when `!ok || !p || !sheet`. It does not draw the legacy ellipse.
- `engine-ab.js` defines the current visual foot anchor as `footY = p.y + 10`.
- `armor-aura.js` wraps the final `renderAvatar()` and draws aura rings/body glow/particles around `p.x/p.y` using `p.radius`; it does not restore a ground shadow.
- `engine-ah.js` explicitly documents that the old bob was removed because it moved “sombra+sprite” together and made the character float. This is strong internal evidence that any new body bob/lean must NOT translate the ground shadow together with the body.
- Architecture/redundancy observation: the shadow behavior still exists only inside a legacy renderer that is bypassed in normal production. Reviving that whole renderer or calling `_av` under the PNG would also revive unrelated legacy body/jewelry/nameplate work and would duplicate responsibilities. The smallest candidate is a dedicated ground-shadow draw, not a renderer rollback.

### EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE
- Godot’s official 2D lighting/shadow documentation states that shadows can materially enhance perceived depth, but also notes that more sophisticated real-time 2D shadow filtering has increasing rendering cost. For Kelo’s grounding cue, this argues for testing the cheapest possible blob/ellipse rather than introducing a full light/occluder pipeline.
- Community top-down 2D practice commonly uses a separate circular/elliptical “blob shadow” under a character as the simplest lightweight shadow. This supports a standalone presentation layer that can remain anchored to the floor independently of body animation.
- A Godot rendering issue (#74265) shows why draw order matters: shadows rendered over their parent sprite produce incorrect composition. This supports an explicit order `ground/world -> blob shadow -> actor body -> front occlusion/UI`, not an effect that is free to wrap above the actor.
- Counterevidence: community feedback also shows that a misplaced or wrongly layered shadow can make a 2D character appear to levitate. Therefore “add a shadow” is not automatically an improvement; the shadow must be tied to the ground/foot root and measured through movement/occlusion crossings.
- Counterevidence: the current art direction may intentionally prefer no blob shadow, or current environmental lighting may already supply enough grounding. A/B screenshots and motion capture must decide this; do not assume the legacy ellipse is artistically correct because it existed before.

### HYPOTHESIS
A subtle, independent blob shadow centered from the hero’s explicit `footRoot` can improve lateral foot planting and scale readability at 1.15x/1.25x with negligible CPU cost, provided it is drawn before the body, remains fixed to the ground when body lean/bob occurs, does not drive collision/depth logic, and does not scale naively with the collider. The best shadow may be smaller/softer than the old radius-based ellipse.

### PROPOSED_CHANGE
Do not restore `engine-d.js::renderAvatar()` and do not call `_av` beneath the PNG during successful rendering. First instrument and compare dedicated shadow candidates inside or immediately before the production actor body pass:

A. `NO_SHADOW_BASELINE`: current production.
B. `LEGACY_GEOMETRY_PROBE`: reproduce only the old ellipse geometry (`centerY=p.y+14`, `rx=.9*radius`, `ry=.45*radius`, alpha .4) before the PNG, to establish whether the old cue was useful; no other legacy renderer code.
C. `FOOTROOT_SHADOW`: center from current explicit visual ground contract (`x=p.x`, Y derived from `footY=p.y+10` with a small measured bias), geometry independent of body bob/lean and collider changes.
D. `PRESENTATION_AWARE_SHADOW`: same ground center, but derive width/height from presentation metadata or a small clamped scale curve so 1.15x/1.25x bodies do not visually outgrow a radius-20 shadow. Do not enlarge collider.

If procedural Canvas2D ellipse edge quality is poor under fractional zoom, compare one tiny pre-authored transparent blob sprite as a rendering-quality diagnostic. Do not introduce dynamic 2D light/shadow casters for this experiment.

### DO_NOT_ASSUME
- Do not assume the old `p.y+14` center is correct for `hero.PNG`; the live sprite’s current `footY` is `p.y+10` and CG-012 is already auditing the foot/depth contract.
- Do not assume shadow size should scale 1:1 with avatar presentation scale.
- Do not attach the shadow transform to body bob, lean, squash, recoil or jump height. The shadow represents the floor contact/reference plane.
- Do not use the shadow for collision, Y-sort, occlusion candidate bounds, or network state.
- Do not revive the old procedural avatar renderer to recover one ellipse.
- Do not add blur/filter/shader work until a plain ellipse/blob proves visible benefit.

### RISKS
- A shadow centered from the wrong vertical origin can worsen the exact “floating” perception it is meant to fix.
- Scaling shadow geometry from the collider alone will look too small as presentation scale grows; scaling it 1:1 with the full body may look too large/heavy.
- Drawing the shadow inside a renderer that can be called multiple times by landmark compositors may duplicate/darken the same shadow in one RAF. Instrument actual `renderAvatar` calls and, if needed, separate shadow/body passes rather than using alpha hacks.
- Aura rings already occupy the lower-body region; a dark blob plus aura can become visually busy at high Aura ranks. Test rank 0 and representative high ranks.
- Fractional world zoom plus `Math.round`/Canvas2D rasterization can make small ellipses shimmer; an authored blob texture may be more stable if the procedural candidate fails.
