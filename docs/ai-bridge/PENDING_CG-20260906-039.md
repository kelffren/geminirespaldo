### PROBLEM
Kelo's avatar presentation contract already exposes a dedicated `shadowAnchorX/Y` equal to the foot root, but the live hero renderer and shared character appearance renderer do not draw or consume a contact shadow. The current visual layer system also has no persistent actor contact-shadow definition in its manifests. That means foot planting is being judged almost entirely from the sprite silhouette against the ground. As we add lateral lean/bob and increase visualScale from 1.15 toward 1.20, the body can visually move more while the ground reference remains unchanged/implicit, making small foot-root or rasterization errors more perceptible.

The goal is not dynamic lighting or a large projected shadow. The research target is a tiny, cheap, actor-local contact/blob shadow anchored to the existing footRoot/shadowAnchor so the body can gain modest secondary motion without visually detaching from the ground.

### CONFIRMED_IN_GEMINI
Current `main` at the start of this round is `cb4659c408c1b81824228bf92b53126db2cc7417`. `index.html` remains Kelo World V6.33 and loads `engine-ab.js`, then `src/characters/character-appearance.js`, then gait/support engines, then the modular visual system and finally late systems including Armor Aura.

`engine-ab.js::presentationOf()` explicitly publishes `shadowAnchorX: footRootX` and `shadowAnchorY: footRootY`, alongside `physicsRoot`, `colliderRadius`, `footRoot`, `depthRoot`, visual bounds and nameplate anchor. However, the live `renderAvatar()` path uses `footRootY` only to place the sprite destination rect and does not render any separate contact shadow. `shadowAnchor` is therefore currently a declared contract with no consumer in the hero renderer.

`src/characters/character-appearance.js` likewise uses `footRootX/Y` to place the sprite and nameplate but does not consume `shadowAnchor` or draw a contact shadow. It also delegates the production player hero back to the legacy/live hero renderer, so it does not add a shadow by another path.

`src/visuals/visual-system.js` defines world layers `groundFX`, `belowActor`, `worldFX`, `foregroundFX` and actor layers `actorBackFX` / `actorFrontFX`, but it does not itself create persistent player shadows. `src/visuals/visual-manifests.js` contains temporary ability rings/glows/status effects, including a ground ring, but no persistent actor contact-shadow manifest. A code search for `shadowAnchor` and generic `shadow` references on current main returned no indexed consumer/result; the relevant owners inspected directly also show no contact-shadow draw.

The current LIVE hero audit verifies sprite preprocessing, visualScale 1.15, footRoot/collider invariants, Aura coupling, RIGHT/LEFT/release/diagonal movement, reversal and plant state, but it records no shadow geometry, shadow/foot overlap, or pixel grounding metric.

This is separate from dynamic 2D lighting. Armor Aura already draws a ring around `p.y + physicsRadius*.72`, and ability VFX can draw ground rings. Those should not be treated as permanent grounding shadows because their presence/size depends on equipment/abilities rather than locomotion.

### EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE
Unity's current URP 2D documentation treats shadow casting as an explicit rendering responsibility with separately configured shadow-caster geometry rather than an incidental property of the gameplay collider. Godot's stable 2D lighting/shadow documentation likewise separates Sprite2D visuals, lights and LightOccluder2D shadow geometry.

For this particular Kelo problem, community practice is more directly relevant: top-down/pixel-art developers commonly use a separate low-alpha blob/contact shadow positioned at the bottom/feet of the character rather than baking grounding into the body sprite. Multiple discussions recommend keeping the shadow as a separate object/layer so its anchor and ordering can be controlled independently. Another community thread on foot sinking recommends bottom/pivot alignment rather than modifying gameplay collision to fix a visual grounding issue.

Counterevidence: full sprite-derived or dynamic projected shadows can be expensive, visually noisy, and can reveal the flatness of 2D sprites. Community reports also note that shadow effects can cost FPS and that moving/rotating projected shadows can look wrong when light direction crosses the actor. Therefore this proposal is deliberately limited to a tiny static contact/blob shadow, not a silhouette projection, normal-map pass, or dynamic 2D light system.

### HYPOTHESIS
A restrained contact shadow anchored exclusively to `KELO_AVATAR_PRESENTATION.shadowAnchor` will improve perceived lateral foot planting and make later 1.20x / lean / bob experiments easier to judge, without changing physics, stride cadence, depth root or camera.

The shadow should represent ground contact, not body scale. Therefore its center should stay exactly at the foot/shadow anchor while its width may scale only modestly with presentation width. It should not bob vertically with the torso, should not mirror on LEFT/RIGHT, and should not be recomputed from per-frame opaque feet unless measurement proves that necessary.

Because 1.20x increases body size, a shadow whose width scales linearly 1.15 -> 1.20 may be acceptable, but a full proportional increase is not assumed. The benchmark should compare fixed-world-size and weakly-scaled variants.

### PROPOSED_CHANGE
Do not change production yet.

First add QA-only shadow instrumentation to `scripts/live-hero-audit.mjs` and/or a guarded renderer hook:
- expose `shadowAnchorX/Y` on every dynamic movement sample;
- record `shadowCenterX/Y`, `shadowRadiusX/Y`, alpha and draw count when a QA contact shadow is enabled;
- verify shadow center does not move relative to footRoot during stride, reversal or release;
- capture fixed crops at RIGHT, LEFT reversal, release and diagonal.

Then test two minimal Canvas2D candidates behind the body:

Candidate A — fixed contact shadow:
- ellipse centered on `shadowAnchorX`, `shadowAnchorY - 1..2` world px;
- width approximately 24-30 world px, height 7-10 px;
- low alpha approximately 0.14-0.22;
- no blur/filter, no dynamic rotation, no per-frame allocation;
- rendered before body/nameplate and before actor-front VFX.

Candidate B — presentation-aware contact shadow:
- same center and alpha discipline;
- width derived weakly from `visualWidth` (for example clamp around 0.45-0.55 * visualWidth), height kept nearly constant;
- compare 1.15 and QA-only 1.20 without changing collider/physics.

Implementation ownership should be decided after measurement. A tiny reusable `renderActorContactShadow(actor, layout)` called around `renderAvatar` is probably cleaner than baking it into `hero.PNG`, but do not refactor renderAvatar/Aura/depth wrappers in the same change. If the modular visual system becomes the owner, ensure actor-local rendering is actually called in the final live chain and does not duplicate on prop redraws (related CG-035).

### DO_NOT_ASSUME
Do not implement dynamic projected sprite shadows, normal maps, light occluders or a full lighting rewrite in this step.
Do not bake the contact shadow into `hero.PNG`; that would couple grounding to animation frames and make footRoot/scale work harder to audit.
Do not move `shadowAnchor` with bob/lean. Secondary body motion should move the body around the ground anchor, not the ground anchor itself.
Do not enlarge `p.radius` or collision geometry to match the shadow.
Do not treat Armor Aura rings or ability ground rings as substitutes for a persistent contact shadow.
Do not render the shadow through a wrapper that can be redrawn multiple times by generic-prop/plaza depth without measuring alpha multiplication first.
Do not assume bigger/darker is better; excessive blob shadows can make sprites look pasted onto the floor.

### EXPERIMENT
Baseline (no shadow):
1. On current 1.15 scale, capture deterministic RIGHT 1.0s, LEFT reversal, release, diagonal and idle crops at mobile 390x844 DPR2 and desktop 1280x720 DPR1.
2. Record footRoot and visual sprite bottom for every sample.
3. Score/measure ground separation around the lower 12-16 screen pixels of the actor crop, especially during frames whose opaque-foot centroid shifts laterally.

Candidate A:
4. Enable QA-only fixed contact shadow with identical physics/world trace.
5. Repeat the exact samples and screenshots.
6. Verify shadow center == presentation shadowAnchor within <=0.25 world px before final raster snapping and that its screen-space center remains stable through RIGHT<->LEFT reversal.

Candidate B:
7. Repeat with weak width scaling from visualWidth.
8. Repeat A/B at QA-only visualScale 1.20 while keeping physics/camera/collider identical.

Stress/performance:
9. Render 1, 8, 16 and 32 actors with the candidate shadow and measure frame time/draw counts. The intended implementation is one cheap ellipse or one tiny cached sprite per visible actor; no allocations per frame.
10. Cross at least one prop redraw/occlusion zone to ensure the shadow is not alpha-multiplied by actor redraw wrappers.

Optional later experiment, only after contact-shadow baseline is good: introduce <=1 px body bob driven by stride phase while shadowAnchor remains fixed. The shadow should make it easier to determine whether the torso motion adds weight without making the feet float.

### DECIDING_METRICS
- `shadowFootAnchorDeltaWorldPx` <= 0.25 (before rasterization; ideally 0 by construction)
- `shadowDirectionFlipCenterDeltaPx` ~= 0 for RIGHT<->LEFT
- `shadowReleaseCenterDriftPx` ~= 0
- `contactGroundingPixelScore` or fixed-crop lower-body diff, baseline vs candidate
- `visibleFloatArtifactCount`
- `shadowDrawCountPerActorFrame` == 1 in ordinary rendering
- no alpha-multiplication in prop-redraw zones
- frame-time delta for 1/8/16/32 actors
- draw-call increment predictable and bounded
- `worldTraceDelta = 0`
- `cameraTraceDelta = 0`
- `colliderDelta = 0`
- `collisionOutcomeDiff = 0`
- `footRootDelta = 0`
- `stridePhaseDelta = 0`
- `nameplatePositionDelta = 0`

A good candidate should improve perceived/contact grounding in fixed crops while leaving all gameplay traces unchanged and adding negligible frame cost.

### RISKS
A shadow that is too large/dark can visually glue Kelo to the floor or obscure foot pixels.
Scaling shadow width directly with visualScale can make 1.20 look heavier even if the body itself is not better; compare fixed vs weakly-scaled shadow.
Rendering through the current `renderAvatar` wrapper chain may duplicate the shadow during actor redraw/occlusion and darken it; measure draw multiplicity before choosing ownership.
Nearest-neighbor + fractional world zoom (CG-033) can make a tiny ellipse/sprite shimmer if its destination is snapped differently from the footRoot. Measure screen-space center fractions rather than adding arbitrary rounding.
If future real lighting is added, a contact shadow should remain a small grounding term or be quality-governed; it should not conflict with projected light shadows.
A global `belowActor` world layer rendered once before all actors is not automatically sufficient for per-actor depth interactions; ordering around actor redraws must be explicit.
