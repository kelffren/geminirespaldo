### PROBLEM
Current main has gained useful lateral-contact telemetry in engine-ab.js: hero preprocessing measures, for each rendered lateral frame, the lowest opaque row plus supportMinX/supportMaxX/supportCentroidX from the bottom three visible silhouette rows. However, the live player renderer still draws every frame by centering the cropped sprite rectangle on p.x/footRootX. Therefore the newly measured support-foot geometry is observational only; it does not yet stabilize the visible support point. This matters before adding body lean, bob, squash, shadow compression or 1.20x scale: secondary motion applied around the sprite center can amplify visible foot slip even while physicsRoot/footRoot remain numerically stable.

### EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE
Unity Blend Tree documentation recommends aligning locomotion clips by normalized foot-contact moments (for example left-foot contact at one phase and right-foot contact at another), which supports treating support contact as an authored animation landmark rather than deriving presentation only from actor-center translation: https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/class-BlendTree.html . Unity animation-curve documentation likewise uses foot-ground states as key animation events/curves. Community 2D guidance consistently identifies visible sliding as a mismatch between the animation's implied step/contact distance and world travel, and recommends keeping the planted foot visually stationary while the body moves over it; a representative discussion is https://www.reddit.com/r/gamedev/comments/o9esl6/ . Another practical counterexample is https://www.reddit.com/r/gamedev/comments/v5p29e/ where bottom/pivot alignment is suggested to prevent feet from visually sinking or drifting.

Counterevidence: supportCentroidX from the bottom three opaque rows is not guaranteed to identify the biomechanically correct planted foot. A frame can contain both feet, coat hems, effects, or asymmetrical pixels near the lowest row. Therefore bottom-band centroid is evidence for a candidate anchor, not an automatic production anchor. Also, perfectly freezing one support pixel can make a stylized sprite look unnaturally rigid if the art intentionally contains controlled foot roll/toe-off. The benchmark must compare measured reduction in slip against visible pose quality rather than optimizing centroid drift to zero blindly.

### HYPOTHESIS
A premium lateral result will come from separating three transforms: (1) stable world footRoot/contact anchor, (2) optional per-frame support compensation derived from validated contact geometry, and only then (3) body-only secondary motion such as tiny lean/bob. If frame-specific support compensation reduces planted-foot screen drift while world/camera/collider traces remain identical, then lean/bob can be applied around the support anchor instead of the sprite center without breaking grounding. The likely highest-value sequence is support-anchor validation -> support compensation -> shadow anchor -> body lean/bob, not lean/bob first.

### RISKS
Bottom-band support detection may lock onto the wrong pixels. Per-frame X compensation can introduce torso/head jitter if the source anchor itself is noisy. LEFT mirroring can invert/offset the correction incorrectly because engine-ab.js currently reflects around p.x while destination X is rounded. Applying lean after compensation can accidentally move the support point if transform order/pivot is wrong. A bob tied to render frame instead of stride phase would reintroduce refresh-rate dependence. At 1.20x, nearest-neighbor quantization can amplify one-world-pixel corrections into visible stepping depending on zoom/DPR. Many actors with per-frame dynamic transforms should be measured, although arithmetic cost is expected to be small compared with sprite draws.
