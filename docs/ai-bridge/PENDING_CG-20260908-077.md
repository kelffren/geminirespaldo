### PROBLEM
The current lateral reversal audit treats reversal continuity mainly as a sprite-column continuity problem. `engine-ac.js` preserves `stridePhase` across LEFT↔RIGHT changes and only increments `reversalFrameJumpCount` when the resulting frame index moves by more than one column. `engine-ab.js`, however, renders both lateral directions from row 2 and obtains LEFT by mirroring the entire RIGHT pose around `p.x`. Therefore a reversal may report zero frame jump while the visible bottom support point moves instantly from one side of the body to the mirrored side. The current audit can pass this case because it does not compare the support point before and after the direction flip.

### CONFIRMED_IN_GEMINI
At base commit `6a603867c8d2346c101e83f6d86d9e6b109dbe54`, `engine-ac.js` detects `lateralReversal` from the horizontal input sign, preserves the existing `stridePhase`, advances it from resolved world distance, and then records only `lastReversalFromFrame`, `lastReversalToFrame`, and `reversalFrameJumpCount`. A frame jump is counted only when circular column distance is greater than one. `engine-ab.js` uses lateral row 2 for both LEFT and RIGHT, with LEFT implemented by `translate(p.x) → scale(-1,1) → translate(-p.x)`. The hero preprocess already publishes per-lateral-frame `supportCentroidX`, `supportMinX`, `supportMaxX`, `supportWidthPx`, and `lowestOpaqueY`, but those values are not connected to reversal QA. `scripts/live-hero-audit.mjs` executes RIGHT→LEFT and asserts `reversalFrameJumpDelta === 0`; it does not measure pre/post reversal support-point displacement. Thus the existing QA proves column continuity, not planted-foot continuity.

### EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE
Godot's AnimationTree documents Sync transitions that switch state while seeking the new animation to the previous playback position, and its current cyclic sync modes explicitly keep locomotion-loop phases aligned. This supports preserving locomotion phase as a reasonable baseline rather than resetting every direction change. Community reports likewise describe frame reset on direction changes causing visible leg jumps/sliding and recommend preserving current animation position. However, Unity documentation on coupling animation and locomotion warns that even blended transitions can produce unnatural foot sliding when the feet are in different positions, showing that matching playback time alone does not guarantee contact continuity. A Godot community discussion also notes that simply flipping a sprite seamlessly continues the animation, but suggests a dedicated pivot/turn animation when a rapid direction change needs more authored weight. Therefore phase preservation is useful but is not sufficient evidence that mirrored support contact remains continuous.

### HYPOTHESIS
For lateral frames whose visible support centroid is not centered under the physics/foot root, instant horizontal mirroring at a reversal causes a screen-space support jump approximately twice the frame's current horizontal support offset from the mirror axis (subject to crop and destination scaling). This can occur even when `lastReversalFromFrame === lastReversalToFrame` and `reversalFrameJumpCount === 0`. The perceptual severity should be largest on strongly asymmetric contact frames and at larger avatar scale/zoom. A contact-aware phase remap or a very short authored/presentation turn bridge may reduce the jump without modifying world movement.

### RISKS
Contact metadata uses a three-pixel bottom silhouette band, which may not always identify the biomechanically intended planted foot; compare centroid with min/max support and screenshots. White knockout/crop can alter measured support geometry. A phase remap may reduce support jump but create a larger torso/arm silhouette pop. A one-frame facing hold can feel laggy at 60 Hz and may behave differently at 120 Hz unless expressed as a presentation condition rather than a time debounce. Dedicated turn art increases asset complexity and should be a last resort. Collider contact may make resolved movement direction differ from input direction, so record both.
