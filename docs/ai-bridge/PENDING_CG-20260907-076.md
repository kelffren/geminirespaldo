### PROBLEM
The current discussion about increasing the hero from visualScale 1.15 toward 1.20 risks focusing on source resolution when the live renderer is already reducing a much larger sprite frame to a small world-space destination rectangle. engine-ab.js disables image smoothing, rounds the destination x/y in world units, and then engine-c.js applies camera/world zoom to the already rounded destination. At non-integer zoom and DPR combinations this can produce uneven nearest-neighbor sampling or temporal edge shimmer even though the source PNG has ample resolution. The QA harness currently asserts scale=1.15 and takes screenshots, but it does not quantify sampling stability, silhouette edge changes, or screen-space pixel-phase jitter.

### CONFIRMED_IN_GEMINI
- main was re-read at commit a8e256b2065d5823c97b068cb384199847ab6785. index.html still declares Kelo World V6.52 and loads engine-a -> engine-c -> engine-h -> engine-z -> engine-ab in that order, with late UI/system wrappers afterward.
- engine-ab.js defines COLS=4, ROWS=4 and initializes FW=256, FH=384 before replacing them on image load with raw.width/4 and raw.height/4. Its authored presentation uses base side width 48 world px, base height round(54*(FH/FW)), then AVATAR_VISUAL_SCALE=1.15. For the 256:384 frame ratio this is about 55x93 world px at 1.15 and about 58x97 at 1.20.
- engine-ab.js crops about 5% horizontally and 4% vertically from each source frame, then drawImage scales that large source rectangle into the much smaller destination rectangle. Therefore moving from 1.15 to 1.20 does not by itself approach a native-source-resolution ceiling; it slightly reduces the amount of downsampling.
- engine-ab.js explicitly sets ctx.imageSmoothingEnabled=false around hero drawImage and rounds only destination x/y with Math.round(...). dw/dh are already integer-rounded by presentationOf.
- engine-c.js establishes the world transform before renderAvatar via translate(screen center) -> scale(CONFIG.zoom) -> translate(-camera). Thus the hero destination rectangle is world-space and is subsequently scaled by CONFIG.zoom.
- engine-h.js configures the HiDPI backing store with ctx.setTransform(dpr,0,0,dpr,0,0), sets canvas.style.imageRendering='pixelated', sets ctx.imageSmoothingEnabled=false, and labels the path hidpi-pixel-perfect-v2. It also tries to make selected zoom targets map to integer physical scales, but engine-z.js loads later and overwrites CONFIG.zoom from viewport width, clamped to 1.05..1.45.
- engine-z.js uses z=screenW/(targetTiles*32) with targetTiles 11 mobile / 14 desktop, then clamps to 1.05..1.45. Consequently final zoom can be non-integer relative to world units and can override engine-h's pixelPerfectZoom default.
- Current live-hero-audit verifies DPR/backing-store dimensions, 1.15 visual scale, footRoot/collider invariants, movement/reversal/release and captures screenshots. It does not measure actual screen-space hero edge phase, per-frame silhouette temporal variance, effective physical-pixel scale, or compare smoothing policies.
- Redundancy/ownership: engine-h advertises a pixel-perfect zoom policy, while later engine-z independently owns the effective auto-zoom. This is a real ownership conflict for sampling quality, but no refactor is proposed until baseline/change/same-trace measurements exist.

### EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE
- MDN documents that CanvasRenderingContext2D.imageSmoothingEnabled controls whether scaled images are smoothed and specifically notes disabling smoothing as useful for pixel art when enlarging images. This supports nearest-neighbor as a deliberate choice, but does not establish that it is optimal for strong non-integer downscaling.
- MDN drawImage documents that destination width/height scale the image, and Canvas scale() further changes the coordinate-to-pixel transform. This matches Kelo's two-stage effective scaling: drawImage source->world destination, then world->screen zoom/DPR transform.
- MDN imageSmoothingQuality is not Baseline across major browsers, so a production solution should not depend on high-quality smoothing support without capability tests.
- Community pixel-art reports repeatedly identify non-integer scale and subpixel camera/sprite relationships as sources of uneven pixels/jitter; a 2025 r/gamedev report shows cardinal/diagonal jitter under point filtering and high refresh, while r/gamemaker discussion notes that non-integer scale distributes source pixels unevenly and that integer-scale intermediate surfaces are a common alternative.
- Counterevidence: nearest-neighbor plus integer-rounded world positions can be aesthetically correct for intentionally crunchy pixel art. Enabling smoothing can make Kelo softer/blurred and may be visually worse. Also Kelo's source artwork is relatively high resolution compared with its display rectangle, so the asset may behave more like illustrated sprite art than classic low-resolution pixel art; the correct filter cannot be chosen by doctrine alone.

### HYPOTHESIS
The first safe increase from 1.15 to 1.20 is unlikely to be limited by hero.PNG resolution or memory. The dominant visual risk is temporal resampling: nearest-neighbor downsampling of a large source into ~55-58 world px, followed by non-integer camera zoom/DPR transforms and integer world-position rounding. A QA-only sampling matrix should identify whether current nearest-neighbor is stable, whether screen-space snapping improves it, or whether a pre-scaled/cache surface yields better edge stability without blur. No production scale increase should be coupled to a filtering change until those variables are separated.

### PROPOSED_CHANGE
Do not change production yet. Benchmark these presentation-only candidates with identical world/input traces:
A) current baseline: imageSmoothing=false, current world x/y rounding, current auto zoom, scale 1.15.
B) scale-only control: exactly current rendering policy but AVATAR_VISUAL_SCALE=1.20. This isolates whether 1.20 itself harms quality.
C) screen-phase control: keep nearest-neighbor and 1.20, but derive final actor screen placement from a consistent physical-pixel/grid policy instead of independently rounding world x/y before camera zoom. Implement QA-only; do not alter physics/camera state.
D) cached pre-scale control: preprocess the active hero frames to an intermediate destination-scale canvas or ImageBitmap at a controlled ratio, then render that cache 1:1 or near-1:1 in world presentation. This tests whether one controlled resample is more stable than large-source nearest-neighbor downsampling every frame.
E) smoothing diagnostic only: enable image smoothing for the hero draw at 1.20 to quantify blur versus temporal shimmer. Do not ship by default; imageSmoothingQuality cannot be assumed cross-browser.

Keep collider radius, physicsRoot, footRoot, depthRoot, nameplate anchor semantics, gait, speed and camera trace invariant. Do not use a lower-resolution replacement asset in this experiment.

### DO_NOT_ASSUME
- Do not call hero.PNG low-resolution merely because the displayed hero is small; verify raw sheet/frame dimensions in the live audit output.
- Do not assume imageSmoothing=false is universally best for downscaling; MDN's pixel-art example primarily demonstrates enlargement.
- Do not assume engine-h's pixelPerfectZoom remains authoritative; engine-z loads later and changes CONFIG.zoom.
- Do not snap physics/world coordinates to device pixels. Any candidate snapping must be presentation-only.
- Do not combine 1.20, new shadow, lean/bob or cadence changes in the same measurement.
- Do not replace Canvas2D with WebGL/WebGPU for this issue before proving Canvas2D sampling is the bottleneck.

A filtering/snap candidate should only advance if it materially reduces temporal shimmer without introducing obvious blur or stepping.

### RISKS
- Screen-space snapping can make movement appear stepped even though physics remains smooth, especially at slow joystick magnitudes.
- Pre-scaled caches add memory and invalidation complexity for appearance/equipment variants; test memory before generalizing.
- Smoothing can hide shimmer while visibly softening the sprite, which is not a true quality win.
- Nearest-neighbor with non-integer zoom can create uneven source-pixel coverage; forcing integer zoom can instead change field of view/composition and is not automatically acceptable.
- engine-h and engine-z currently have overlapping zoom responsibilities; changing one without tracing final CONFIG.zoom risks false conclusions.
- Browser implementations can differ in image resampling behavior, especially with imageSmoothingQuality; test Chromium mobile/desktop first and add Safari/WebKit before declaring universal behavior.
