### PROBLEM
The previous camera pass treated the core Canvas backing store and `roundPixels=false` defaults as if they remained active. Current LIVE ownership disproves that assumption: `engine-h.js` is loaded later and replaces resize with a DPR-aware backing store (cap 3), forces `CONFIG.roundPixels=true`, and computes a DPR-aligned pixel-perfect zoom. Then `engine-z.js`, loaded still later, overwrites `CONFIG.zoom` from viewport width with a generally fractional value and reduces look-ahead to 36. This means the runtime is already HiDPI and rounded, but the later zoom owner can defeat the earlier integer-physical-scale intent. Separately, `performance-governor.js` evaluates health against a fixed 60fps target, so it cannot tell whether lateral motion is actually meeting a 90Hz or 120Hz display cadence.

### CONFIRMED_IN_GEMINI
- Current `main` head at the start of this pass: `ddac5184f6e2468204ae36aed10bf52311996d4b`.
- `index.html` declares V6.23 and loads `engine-h.js` before `engine-t.js`, `engine-z.js`, `engine-ab.js`, and `engine-ac.js`; therefore later wrappers/assignments win.
- `engine-h.js` defines `activeDpr() = min(devicePixelRatio, 3)`, replaces `resize()` so canvas backing dimensions are `screenW*dpr` / `screenH*dpr`, applies `ctx.setTransform(dpr,...)`, disables smoothing, sets `CONFIG.roundPixels=true`, and defines `pixelPerfectZoom(target)` by rounding `target*dpr` to an integer physical scale.
- `engine-z.js` later computes `CONFIG.zoom = clamp(screenW/(targetTiles*32), 1.05, 1.45)` and, if look-ahead exceeds 40, sets it to 36. This zoom is not quantized through `engine-h.js::pixelPerfectZoom()`.
- `engine-c.js` renders the world under `ctx.scale(CONFIG.zoom, CONFIG.zoom)`, so the effective physical world scale is approximately `DPR * CONFIG.zoom`. If that product is non-integer, authored pixels can map to uneven physical-pixel widths while the camera/player are moving laterally.
- `engine-t.js` also writes `CONFIG.zoom`, but it loads before `engine-z.js`; its clamp is therefore not the final zoom owner on startup.
- `engine-ac.js` does not override zoom, DPR, `roundPixels`, or camera damping. Its distance-driven stride remains independent of render frame count.
- `src/systems/performance-governor.js` has `TARGET_FPS=60`, initializes target frame time to 16.67ms, marks healthy above 58fps/<17ms, and runs its own rAF sampler. On a 90/120Hz display, a cadence materially below panel refresh can still be classified healthy by this governor.
- `engine-ab.js` already rounds avatar destination coordinates and disables image smoothing. These controls do not guarantee temporal stability if the outer camera/world transform uses a non-integer physical zoom.
- Relevant ownership is duplicated across `engine-h.js`, `engine-t.js`, and `engine-z.js`: three modules assign zoom policy, with the final runtime behavior determined by script order rather than one explicit contract. This is a real responsibility overlap; do not refactor it blindly before A/B measurement.

### EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE
- MDN `devicePixelRatio`: high-DPI mobile displays commonly exceed DPR 2, and the recommended sharp Canvas pattern is to enlarge the backing store by DPR and normalize the context transform. This confirms that `engine-h.js`, not the core resize path, is the relevant LIVE HiDPI owner. https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/devicePixelRatio
- MDN Canvas optimization guidance likewise recommends DPR-aware backing stores for high-resolution displays, while warning that Canvas work has real rendering cost. https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Canvas_API/Tutorial/Optimizing_canvas
- MDN `imageSmoothingEnabled`: disabling smoothing retains hard pixel edges when images are enlarged, but it does not promise that fractional transforms will be temporally stable. https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/CanvasRenderingContext2D/imageSmoothingEnabled
- MDN `requestAnimationFrame`: callbacks generally follow display refresh and 60/75/120/144Hz are common; progression and measurements must use timing rather than frame count. https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/requestAnimationFrame
- PixiJS issue #6676 documents nearest-neighbor artifacts that appear only at specific scaled/container positions, supporting the need to benchmark physical-scale alignment instead of assuming nearest filtering alone is pixel-perfect. https://github.com/pixijs/pixijs/issues/6676
- Phaser issue #7188 reports pixel-art jitter at low resolution/slow movement even with pixel-art settings, again showing that filtering/rounding flags alone do not guarantee smooth motion. https://github.com/phaserjs/phaser/issues/7188
- Community counterevidence: a recent Godot pixel-art jitter thread reports that integer scaling/filtering changes do not universally remove perceived jitter; update-loop alignment and display/motion cadence can matter too. Treat this as anecdotal evidence, not engine-specific proof for Gemini. https://www.reddit.com/r/godot/comments/1veudr7/my_pixel_art_2d_character_will_not_stop_jittering/

### HYPOTHESIS
A meaningful share of remaining lateral shimmer/"not planted" perception may come from the combination of integer avatar destination rounding with a later viewport-derived zoom whose `DPR*zoom` is fractional. Before changing gait/body animation again, benchmark a single zoom-ownership candidate that preserves current framing as closely as possible while quantizing physical scale. In parallel, make the benchmark refresh-aware: infer observed panel cadence from rAF intervals and judge missed-vsync/frame pacing relative to 60/90/120Hz instead of relying on the governor's fixed 60fps health label.

### RISKS
At DPR 2 the backing store has ~4x the CSS pixel count; at DPR 3 it has ~9x, so high-DPR crispness can materially raise fill cost on mobile. Quantizing zoom can change world framing in noticeable steps, especially on narrow screens. Fixed 60fps governor thresholds can hide refresh-relative stutter, but changing governor behavior prematurely could also cause needless quality drops on 120Hz phones. Rounding camera/player/world transforms at multiple levels may introduce stepwise motion even if individual frames are crisp. Consolidating zoom owners without a baseline could silently break touch coordinate mapping (`screenToWorld`), camera framing, or UI expectations.
