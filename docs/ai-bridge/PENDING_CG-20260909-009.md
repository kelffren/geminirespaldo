### PROBLEM
Before raising the avatar from 1.15x to 1.20x, the current presentation contract needs a silhouette/bounds audit. `KELO_AVATAR_PRESENTATION` publishes fixed direction-class bounds (`visualWidth` 55 for LEFT/RIGHT, 62 for UP/DOWN and ~93 current visualHeight), but Character Appearance does not draw a 55px-wide lateral sprite. It scales the full authored 256x384 frame by `targetBodyHeight / bodyHeight`, and `bodyHeight` differs strongly by direction. At the current ~93px target body height this produces destination-frame widths of about 78.8px LEFT and 64.5px RIGHT; the same `visualWidth=55` contract is therefore not the actual draw rectangle and the LEFT/RIGHT destination geometry differs by ~22%. Transparent frame padding may explain part or all of that difference, so this is NOT proof that the visible opaque silhouette is 78.8px vs 64.5px. It is proof that the current contract is not derived from measured per-frame visible alpha bounds.

This matters because CG-003 proposed moving visual occlusion decisions away from collider radius toward presentation bounds. If the presentation bounds themselves are only coarse constants, a future 1.20x pass can still miss hair/arms/equipment or over-trigger redraws. It also matters for shadow sizing, culling, depth-adjacent effects and nameplate clearance. Separately, the same LIVE renderer downsamples the 256x384 authored frame to roughly 25–31% linear scale with `imageSmoothingEnabled=false`; 1.20x remains a substantial downscale, so visual quality should be benchmarked rather than assuming nearest-neighbor is optimal for this high-resolution authored hero.

### CONFIRMED_IN_GEMINI
Current `main` at BASE_COMMIT:
- `index.html` is Kelo World V6.54 and loads `engine-ab.js` followed by `src/characters/character-appearance.js`; `engine-h.js` configures the camera/Canvas for pixel-perfect compatibility with `smoothing:false` and `imageRendering:'pixelated'`.
- `engine-ab.js` defines `AVATAR_VISUAL_SCALE=1.15`. Its `presentationOf()` publishes `baseVisualWidth=48` for lateral / 54 cardinal, then rounds to `visualWidth≈55` lateral and `visualHeight≈93` at current scale. It publishes `visualLeft/Top/Right/Bottom` from these constants.
- `character-appearance.js` defines an authored sheet of 4x4 frames, each 256x384, and validates the full source as 1024x1536.
- Per-face `bodyHeight` metrics are DOWN 303, LEFT 302, RIGHT 369, UP 307. The renderer computes `scale = layout.visualHeight / metrics.bodyHeight` and then `dw = round(256*scale)`, `dh = round(384*scale)`.
- At current visualHeight≈93 this implies approximate unrounded destination-frame widths: LEFT 78.8, RIGHT 64.5, DOWN 78.6, UP 77.6 world px. The published contract remains 55 lateral / 62 cardinal. The lateral destination-frame width difference is about 22% even though the contract gives LEFT and RIGHT the same visual width.
- The renderer anchors each frame at authored per-frame footX/footY, which is good for foot planting, but it does not currently publish or consume measured alpha-visible min/max bounds per face/frame.
- `character-appearance.js` disables smoothing for the draw, while `KeloCamera`/`engine-h` also configure the main Canvas with smoothing disabled. This renderer therefore downsamples the high-resolution source with a nearest/pixelated policy rather than a filtered high-quality downscale.
- At a hypothetical 1.20x, the presentation visualHeight would become about 97 world px; LEFT and RIGHT full-frame destination widths would become about 82.2 and 67.3 respectively. The relative direction mismatch persists, and visible edge errors become easier to notice because the avatar occupies more screen area.
- No production code was changed in this research pass.

### EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE
- MDN documents `imageSmoothingEnabled=false` primarily as useful for preserving sharp pixel-art pixels when scaling; it does not claim that nearest-neighbor is universally best for downscaling detailed high-resolution art. MDN also exposes `imageSmoothingQuality` for smoothed scaling, but notes that support is not Baseline across all major browsers, so a production policy cannot rely blindly on `high` everywhere.
- Godot Sprite2D exposes `get_rect()` for sprite bounds and `is_pixel_opaque()` for pixel-level visibility checks. This reinforces the distinction between a sprite's render region/bounds and unrelated physics geometry.
- PixiJS culling uses renderer/global screen bounds and permits an explicit custom `cullArea` when default bounds are expensive or inaccurate. This supports measuring conservative presentation bounds instead of substituting collider radius.
- W3C/CSSWG issue #5837 documents distortion risk from nearest-neighbor/pixelated scaling at non-integer scale factors; the broader point is that a pixelated policy is safest when source/output grids align and can become visually irregular at arbitrary fractional scales.
- A 2025 Godot community report describes downscaled pixel-art sprites losing edge pixels depending on position at small resolutions; commenters point out the unavoidable trade-off: at a smaller output resolution either detail is discarded or interpolation blends it. This is community evidence, not proof for Kelo, but it is directly relevant because Kelo is reducing 302–369 authored body pixels to ~93 output world pixels.
- Counterevidence: if `hero.PNG` is intentionally authored as pixel art whose desired look is nearest-neighbor, smoothing can blur edges and be strictly worse. Likewise, the full-frame destination rectangle is not the same as the opaque silhouette; transparent margins can make a 79px destination frame perfectly compatible with a ~55px visible body. Therefore neither filtering nor bounds should change until alpha-visible output is measured.

### HYPOTHESIS
The current 55px/62px presentation widths are coarse semantic placeholders rather than trustworthy visual silhouette bounds. A per-face/per-frame conservative alpha-bound contract, measured once from the cleaned sprite source and transformed through the existing foot anchor/scale, will make occlusion/culling/shadow/nameplate work safer at 1.15x and 1.20x without touching collider or physics. Separately, the current nearest-neighbor downscale may or may not be visually optimal; a controlled filtered-downscale comparison can determine whether the detailed hero benefits from smoothing while preserving crispness at rest.

### DO_NOT_ASSUME

- Do not assume `visualWidth=55` is wrong merely because `dw≈79` LEFT; transparent source padding may reconcile them.
- Do not increase collider radius to match art.
- Do not derive visual bounds from `actor.radius`.
- Do not introduce per-frame `getImageData()` in the hot render loop; alpha bounds should be measured once at asset load/preprocess and cached.

### RISKS
- Per-frame tight bounds can create occlusion/culling chatter as arms/legs animate; a face-level union may be safer than exact per-frame bounds.
- Preprocessing alpha bounds adds startup work, although it can reuse the cleanup canvas already created once per appearance.
- Smoothing the hero locally can create a style mismatch against pixelated environment art.
- Nearest-neighbor at arbitrary fractional downscale can discard details differently by position/frame; smoothing can instead blur silhouettes. Both are plausible failure modes.
- A 1.20x avatar will magnify any bounds/filter asymmetry and may expose errors that are hard to notice at 1.15x.
- If modular Character Customization adds layers extending outside the base body, base-hero bounds alone are insufficient; the final contract must eventually union active appearance layers without scanning pixels every frame.
