### PROBLEM
The current lateral movement investigation has improved pose/stride/grounding contracts, but Kelo's camera can still make a mechanically correct side-walk feel less planted because its dead-zone is computed from CSS viewport dimensions and then compared directly against world-space player/camera deltas. Since engine-c.js subsequently scales the entire world by CONFIG.zoom, the effective dead-zone on the display grows with zoom. The effect is materially different between the current mobile and desktop framing, so the same character movement can produce different screen-space drift before the camera reacts. This should be benchmarked before attributing remaining lateral softness to sprite animation or increasing the avatar to 1.20x.

### CONFIRMED_IN_GEMINI
- Current main was re-read at BASE_COMMIT. The commit immediately before this entry only restored assets/fx/sword-swap/.keep.PNG; it did not change engine-a.js, engine-c.js, engine-z.js, engine-ab.js or engine-ac.js compared with the movement code inspected in this round.
- engine-a.js updateCamera(dt) computes deadW = screenW * CONFIG.deadXRatio and deadH = screenH * CONFIG.deadYRatio, then compares those values directly to deltaX/deltaY where player, lookOffset and camera target are world coordinates.
- engine-c.js applies world rendering as translate(screen center) -> scale(CONFIG.zoom) -> translate(-camera), proving that camera/player deltas are world-space while the final display displacement is multiplied by zoom.
- engine-z.js is the late zoom owner for normal framing: targetTiles=11 below 500px width, otherwise 14; CONFIG.zoom is clamped to 1.05..1.45. It also clamps CONFIG.lookAheadDist from the engine-a default 60 down to 36 world units.
- engine-a.js defaults deadXRatio=0.10, deadYRatio=0.08, dampX=dampY=8, lookAheadDecay=4. engine-ac.js changes movement speed/input policy but does not redefine those camera dead-zone/damping ratios.
- At the existing hero audit viewports, the resulting zoom/dead-zone math is approximately:
  - mobile 390x844: zoom = 390/(11*32) = 1.10795; horizontal dead threshold = 39 world px = 43.2 CSS px after zoom, or 11.08% of screen width per side; vertical threshold = 67.52 world = 74.8 CSS px.
  - desktop 1280x720: zoom clamps to 1.45; horizontal dead threshold = 128 world = 185.6 CSS px, or 14.5% of screen width per side; vertical threshold = 57.6 world = 83.5 CSS px.
  Thus the nominal `deadXRatio=0.10` does not remain a 10% screen-space margin once zoom != 1.
- The same mismatch exists in the world-edge camera clamp: engine-a.js uses halfW=screenW/2 and halfH=screenH/2 as world-space camera limits even though the visible half-extent under zoom is screenW/(2*zoom), screenH/(2*zoom). At desktop zoom 1.45, the horizontal clamp uses 640 world px instead of about 441.4, keeping the camera ~198.6 world px farther from the map edge than the actual viewport requires.
- The exponential camera smoothing itself is refresh-rate-aware: both camera damping and look-ahead easing use 1-exp(-k*dt), so this round does not identify a render-count/Hz bug in those two equations.
- scripts/live-hero-audit.mjs currently records player/movement/presentation but not camera position, camera target, look offset, CONFIG.zoom, or player screen-space offset. Therefore its RIGHT/LEFT trace cannot currently distinguish an animation issue from a camera-composition issue.

### EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE
- Unity Cinemachine Position Composer defines dead zone and soft zone explicitly in frame/screen composition space: the target can move inside a region of the frame without reframing, and damping controls how the camera responds after it enters the soft zone. This supports keeping the camera contract stable in screen space even when zoom changes. Source: https://docs.unity.cn/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@6.6/manual/CinemachinePositionComposer.html
- Godot Camera2D documents drag margins as values relative to screen size; values near 0 keep the camera closer to center and values near 1 allow movement toward screen edges. This is additional engine-level evidence that a screen-composition margin should remain a screen-relative quantity rather than accidentally scaling with world zoom. Source: https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/classes/class_camera2d.html
- Godot's documentation issue #7774 collects many 2D camera-jitter cases and specifically notes that a small drag margin can avoid smoothing jitter near the camera destination. This supports retaining a dead zone rather than removing it completely. Source: https://github.com/godotengine/godot-docs/issues/7774
- Godot issue #35606 documents visible one-pixel sprite jitter during smooth Camera2D movement, reinforcing that camera behavior can masquerade as a sprite-quality problem. Source: https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/35606
- Counterevidence: community reports show that aggressive pixel snapping/rounding can itself make smooth/diagonal motion jerky, and Godot issue #98764 shows transform snapping can produce particle jitter. Therefore this proposal does NOT round the camera or force integer transforms. Source: https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/98764
- Community feedback on camera look-ahead also warns that overly fast or overly large offset catch-up can feel uncomfortable or induce motion sickness, so look-ahead should be measured separately rather than bundled into the dead-zone fix. Example: https://www.reddit.com/r/IndieDev/comments/17iv3ci/why_does_my_camera_lookahead_feel_off/

### HYPOTHESIS
Part of the perceived lateral looseness is presentation-level camera composition, not locomotion. If the intended dead zone is a stable fraction of the visible frame, converting that screen-space margin into world units with division by CONFIG.zoom should make mobile and desktop preserve the same apparent framing while leaving physics, stride, foot planting and camera smoothing equations unchanged. A second, independent candidate may normalize look-ahead to screen-space too, but that must not be combined with the first test. Fixing the world-edge half-extents is also likely correct mathematically, but should be benchmarked separately because it changes what can be seen near map borders.

### PROPOSED_CHANGE
Do not change production blindly. Extend the hero/camera audit first, then test isolated candidates.

Candidate A — dead-zone coordinate normalization only:
- Keep CONFIG.deadXRatio/deadYRatio unchanged.
- Compute visible world viewport: viewWorldW = screenW / zoom; viewWorldH = screenH / zoom.
- Compute deadW = viewWorldW * deadXRatio; deadH = viewWorldH * deadYRatio.
- Do NOT alter damping, lookAheadDist, lookAheadDecay, movement speeds, stride, sprite scale or zoom in the same candidate.

Candidate B — only after A is measured:
- Decide whether `lookAheadDist` is intentionally world-distance or screen-composition distance. If product intent is stable screen composition, introduce an explicit screen-space look-ahead target and convert to world units by /zoom. Do not infer this silently from the old 36-world-px constant.

Candidate C — edge clamp correction, independently benchmarked:
- Clamp camera center using halfWorldW = screenW/(2*zoom), halfWorldH = screenH/(2*zoom), not screenW/2 and screenH/2.
- Test near all four world bounds and verify no authored map/occlusion assumption depended on the oversized legacy margin.

### DO_NOT_ASSUME
- Do not assume a smaller screen-space dead zone automatically feels better; the current larger desktop zone may have been visually tolerated and the baseline may win.
- Do not remove the dead zone. External evidence supports a small margin as useful for compositional stability and jitter avoidance.
- Do not round camera.x/camera.y or force integer movement as part of this experiment.
- Do not change zoom, avatar scale, foot pivots, stride timing, bob/lean or joystick curves in the same A/B.
- Do not change look-ahead in Candidate A. Dead-zone and look-ahead are coupled perceptually but must be isolated experimentally.
- Do not claim 60/90/120 equivalence solely because the exponential formula is time-based; measure traces at multiple refresh cadences because dt spikes and browser scheduling can still matter.

### RISKS
- A camera that follows earlier can make the world feel more active or reveal raster jitter that the oversized dead zone previously masked; that would expose another problem, not prove dead-zone normalization is wrong.
- Near world edges, correcting the half-extents may expose authored environment areas that were rarely visible under the oversized clamp; world coverage must be inspected.
- A stable screen-space dead zone can still feel different on a physically small phone versus a large monitor because angular size/viewing distance differs; mathematical consistency is not automatic perceptual optimality.
- The current look-ahead of 36 world px partly compensates for the dead zone. Changing both at once could produce misleading results.
- main is moving quickly and current CI has unrelated asset-work failures; re-read main before any implementation and do not use an unrelated failed workflow as evidence against this camera hypothesis.
