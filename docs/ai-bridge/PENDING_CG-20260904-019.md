### PROBLEM
The current production hero renderer has no body-local presentation transform. `engine-ab.js` draws the hero sprite and then the nameplate in the same `renderAvatar()` call. `armor-aura.js`, loaded later, wraps that full call with back/front aura passes. A generic `renderAvatar` translate/rotate for lateral lean would therefore risk moving/rotating the nameplate and, depending on wrapper order, aura/shadow-like ground effects as well. This would repeat the architectural failure behind the historical bob experiment, which was removed because it moved ground reference + sprite together and made Kelo appear to float.

### CONFIRMED_IN_GEMINI
- Current `main` title is Kelo World V6.23 and loads `engine-ab.js?v=240`, then `engine-ac.js?v=220`, then later `armor-aura.js?v=1`.
- `ENGINE_MAP.md` still marks `engine-ab.js + assets/hero.PNG` as OWNER LIVE for the visible hero and `engine-ac.js` as OWNER LIVE for gait/visual motion.
- `engine-ab.js` computes `footY = p.y + 10`, mirrors LEFT by translating around `p.x` and scaling X by -1, then directly draws the sprite. It restores the context and subsequently draws the nameplate. There is no body-local lean/rotation/bob presentation transform or explicit visual pivot object.
- `armor-aura.js` installs a later wrapper: `renderBack(ctx,p); base(p,isSelf); renderFront(ctx,p);`. Aura geometry is based on `p.x/p.y/p.radius`, not the rendered sprite body transform.
- `engine-ah.js` explicitly documents that the prior bob was removed because it moved `shadow+sprite` together and created floating. Git history commit `983bc946...` confirms removal of a stride-phase sinusoidal bob wrapper that translated the entire `renderAvatar()` call.
- Current LEFT is produced from the RIGHT atlas row by canvas mirroring. Any new lean transform must be tested with transform order/sign under this mirror or LEFT/RIGHT can become asymmetric.
- No current code search result establishes a separate body presentation transform owner or a `presentationScale`/`bodyLean` contract.

### EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE
- MDN Canvas2D documents that `rotate()` rotates around the canvas origin; rotating around a character foot/pivot requires translating the origin to that pivot, rotating, then translating back. This supports a foot-root-local transform rather than a whole-renderer transform.
- MDN documents `imageSmoothingEnabled=false` as the appropriate nearest-style behavior for enlarged pixel art, but this only controls filtering; it does not guarantee that arbitrary rotated silhouettes remain stable.
- Godot issue #57221 reports jittery pixel shifts when pixel-perfect sprites combine rotation with float motion. This is not proof of a Canvas2D bug in Kelo, but it is direct counterevidence against assuming a tiny runtime rotation is visually free.
- PixiJS issue #11338 documents jagged edges on rotated images/sprites; again not the same renderer, but useful counterevidence that rotation can degrade silhouette quality even with otherwise correct resolution/filtering.
- Pixel-art community discussion commonly treats runtime rotation as a style tradeoff rather than an automatic upgrade; crisp authored frame changes can be preferable to rotated pixels.

### HYPOTHESIS
A small body-only lateral anticipation/lean may improve perceived weight during RIGHT/LEFT acceleration and hard reversals, but runtime rotation may cost more silhouette stability than it gains. A translation-only or authored-frame-driven lean may beat rotation. The best candidate should keep `footRoot`, world position, collider, nameplate, aura and any ground shadow invariant while changing only the visible body silhouette.

### PROPOSED_CHANGE
Do not add a global `renderAvatar` transform. First introduce only an instrumented body-presentation candidate inside `engine-ab.js` around the `ctx.drawImage()` operation, using `footY` as the mathematical pivot contract and leaving the nameplate outside the transform. Do not change production defaults until measured.

Benchmark candidates:
A. `NO_LEAN_BASELINE` — current production.
B. `BODY_TRANSLATE_ONLY` — at most ~0.5–1.5 world px horizontal/vertical presentation offset derived from signed lateral intent/speed, no rotation, body only, foot-root error explicitly measured.
C. `FOOT_PIVOT_ROTATE` — very small body-only rotation around `(p.x, footY)`; sweep approximately 0.5°, 1.0°, 1.5° maximum rather than assuming more is better. LEFT/RIGHT signs must be validated after the current mirror transform.
D. `POSE_ONLY` — no geometric transform; rely on authored sprite cadence/frame choice for weight if B/C fail quality metrics.

If any lean needs temporal filtering, it must use `dt`/time constants and be verified at 60/90/120 Hz. Do not use render-count increments.

### DO_NOT_ASSUME
- Do not assume rotation looks more premium than no rotation.
- Do not assume `(p.x, footY)` is the exact opaque foot-contact pixel for every lateral frame; classify/measure the atlas first.
- Do not rotate the nameplate, aura, collider, camera, foreground/depth bounds or a future ground shadow with the body.
- Do not revive the historical whole-avatar sinusoidal bob; that path was explicitly removed.
- Do not couple presentation scale or collider radius to lean.
- Do not refactor `renderAvatar` wrappers merely to make the experiment convenient; baseline → candidate → same trace → re-measure first.

### EXPERIMENT
1. Instrument baseline with no visual change. Record physical `(x,y)`, `footY`, selected frame/face/gait, projected foot position, body destination rect, aura centers and nameplate anchor.
2. Use traces: idle→RIGHT walk, RIGHT walk→run, RIGHT run→release, RIGHT run→hard LEFT, LEFT mirror, analog slow lateral, 45° diagonals and wall-slide corrections.
3. Run each candidate at 60/90/120 Hz and zoom/DPR combinations already used by CG-016; test avatar scale 1.00 first, then only the winner at 1.15 and 1.25.
4. For rotation, capture frame-by-frame silhouette masks or edge-change counts and compare LEFT vs RIGHT symmetry under mirroring.
5. Repeat with Aura rank 0 and high Aura. Confirm aura center/rings do not inherit body lean.
6. If CG-013 shadow candidate exists in the test branch, confirm shadow world position remains invariant while body lean changes.
7. Do not alter physics/camera/collisions during these tests.

### RISKS
- Runtime rotation can cause jagged/pixel-crawl artifacts, especially with float motion and fractional zoom/DPR.
- Wrong transform order with the current LEFT mirror can make one direction lean opposite or with a different apparent pivot.
- Rotating around the destination rect bottom center may not equal the opaque foot-contact point due to transparent padding/cropping.
- A wrapper-level implementation can accidentally rotate/move the nameplate, aura and later ground-shadow passes.
- Excess lean can read as skating/falling, particularly during hard reversal or collision wall-slide.
- Additional per-actor save/translate/rotate/restore operations are probably cheap but must still be measured at 50 actors.
