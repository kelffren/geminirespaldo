### PROBLEM
`engine-ac.js` correctly advances stride by actual world distance, but it discards every individual update whose displacement is not greater than `MIN_VISUAL_MOVE_PX = 0.12`. Because the threshold is applied per update rather than to accumulated distance over time, the minimum physical speed that can advance the sprite cycle rises with update frequency. Slow analog touch movement can therefore move Kelo through the world while leaving the walk frame/stridePhase frozen, and the affected range is larger at 90/120 Hz than at 60 Hz.

This is especially relevant to the user's priority of premium lateral motion and real mobile joystick behavior: the current desktop keyboard audit always drives full digital magnitude, so it cannot expose this slow-walk/high-refresh defect.

### CONFIRMED_IN_GEMINI
1. Current `main` was re-read at `98dac7b3d48de3624928c1d5c547bb49395763c4`; no newer Grok response exists beyond `GC-20260831-003`.
2. `index.html` still declares V6.33 and loads `engine-ac.js` after `engine-ab.js`; the active gait owner remains `engine-ac.js`.
3. `engine-ac.js` defines `MIN_VISUAL_MOVE_PX = 0.12` and computes `dist = hypot(p.x-v.lastX,p.y-v.lastY)` each update.
4. It sets `v.lastStepDistancePx = dist > MIN_VISUAL_MOVE_PX ? dist : 0` and advances `strideDistancePx`, `stridePhase`, and `frame` only when that value is greater than zero.
5. Physical movement in `engine-a.js` remains `localPlayer.x += vx * dt` / `y += vy * dt`; therefore a legitimate small `dt` at a high refresh rate naturally produces a smaller per-update distance while preserving the same world speed.
6. With the current threshold, a constant speed must exceed about 7.2 world px/s at 60 updates/s, 10.8 px/s at 90, and 14.4 px/s at 120 for every update to clear 0.12 px.
7. In the current low-magnitude walk range `speedFor(mag)=110`, those speeds correspond to processed joystick magnitudes of about 0.0655, 0.0982 and 0.1309 respectively. With the current 4.5% joystick deadzone and LINEAR mapping, the approximate raw-stick radii are 10.8%, 13.9% and 17.0% of full radius.
8. Therefore there is a concrete band where the same analog intent can animate at 60 Hz but visually freeze at 120 Hz while physics continues moving.
9. `visualOn` does not solve this: `hasIntent` keeps the motion state on, but discarded sub-threshold distances do not accumulate into stride phase.
10. `scripts/live-hero-audit.mjs` uses keyboard `d/a/s` even in its mobile context and samples only full digital gait. It does not drive controlled analog magnitudes and does not emulate 60/90/120-Hz update cadence, so this defect is currently untested.
11. This is not a reason to abandon distance-driven cadence. The defect is the per-update epsilon/discard policy around the otherwise correct distance-driven model.

### EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE
- MDN documents that `requestAnimationFrame()` callback frequency generally follows display refresh rate and explicitly lists 60, 75, 120 and 144 Hz as common; it warns animation progress must use elapsed time rather than assume a fixed per-frame amount: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/requestAnimationFrame
- GitHub issue SardineFish/raindrop-fx#17 reports the same general class of bug: animation behavior tied to a per-frame assumption changes on high-refresh monitors/mobile devices: https://github.com/SardineFish/raindrop-fx/issues/17
- Community gamedev guidance consistently separates sprite animation timing from render FPS; a 2D animation can intentionally run at its authored pose cadence while the game/render loop runs much faster: https://www.reddit.com/r/gamedev/comments/12ve2jc/
- Counterevidence: removing all motion epsilon can make accumulated floating-point/collision micro-jitter advance feet while visually stationary. The replacement should retain noise rejection, but apply it to an accumulated distance/time or velocity-informed state rather than discarding every small update independently.
- Counterevidence: fixed-timestep/interpolation is not automatically superior for pixel-art presentation; community reports show interpolation can itself expose subpixel jitter. This proposal does not request a fixed-timestep rewrite.

### HYPOTHESIS
The slowest part of Kelo's analog walk feels less planted and less consistent on high-refresh phones because valid world displacement is being thrown away before stride accumulation. Converting the epsilon from a per-update gate into an accumulator/noise-floor contract will make a given world trajectory produce nearly identical stride phase at 60/90/120 Hz without changing physics speed, input latency, collider, camera or authored 4-frame cadence.

### PROPOSED_CHANGE
Do not change production first.

Phase 1 — extend QA telemetry with `rawDist`, `acceptedStrideDist`, `discardedStrideDist`, cumulative accepted/discarded distance, update interval, and effective update Hz.

Phase 2 — run controlled analog lateral traces at processed magnitudes 0.05, 0.08, 0.10, 0.13, 0.20, 0.50 and 1.00 under controlled 60/90/120-Hz simulation/update cadence. Measure world distance and stride phase over the same wall-clock duration.

Candidate A — cumulative epsilon bucket: accumulate all positive world displacement into a small pending-distance bucket; only suppress it as noise when the actor is genuinely stationary/blocked. Once cumulative pending distance exceeds the noise threshold, feed the entire accumulated distance into stride phase so no legitimate path length is permanently lost.

Candidate B — lower/velocity-scaled epsilon only if A proves unnecessarily complex. Do not simply divide 0.12 by an assumed FPS constant, because actual refresh/update cadence is variable and dropped frames exist.

### DO_NOT_ASSUME
- Do not make sprite animation time-driven again; preserve distance-driven gait.
- Do not alter physics speed to make individual frames exceed 0.12 px.
- Do not add keyboard acceleration to hide the issue.
- Do not remove collision/noise rejection entirely.
- Do not combine this benchmark with 1.20x scaling, camera deadzone, bob, lean, mirroring or aura-layer changes.
- Do not claim 120-Hz behavior from viewport/DPR alone; control or instrument update cadence explicitly.

### EXPERIMENT
Baseline A:
1. For each controlled update rate 60/90/120, hold pure RIGHT analog input at processed magnitudes 0.05/0.08/0.10/0.13/0.20 for 2 seconds.
2. Record true world distance, sum of raw per-update `dist`, accepted stride distance, discarded distance, stridePhase/frame transitions, and effective gait.
3. Repeat LEFT and a low-speed diagonal to ensure the issue is not direction-specific.

Candidate B — accumulated-distance epsilon:
4. Replay exactly the same trajectories/update schedules.
5. Require identical world positions/collision outcomes to baseline.
6. Compare final stridePhase by normalized world distance rather than render-frame count.
7. Add a stationary-noise control and a blocked-against-wall control; neither should cause creeping stride animation.

Acceptance target for a candidate:
- same world trajectory yields effectively the same accumulated stride distance and final phase at 60/90/120 Hz;
- no legitimate traveled distance is permanently discarded solely because updates are more frequent;
- stationary and blocked controls do not creep through the walk cycle;
- no physics/input/camera/collider change.

### RISKS
- Naively accumulating collision-resolution jitter could animate feet while pressed against a wall; pair the bucket with achieved-motion semantics and the blocked-motion research from CG-030.
- Releasing a stored bucket all at once can cause a one-frame phase jump; if observed, preserve total distance while draining it in a bounded way or lower the accumulation threshold.
- Browser timing is not a perfect constant 60/90/120 Hz; record actual dt/update intervals instead of trusting requested cadence.
- Very slow analog motion may intentionally deserve an idle/creep pose rather than a full walk cycle. That is an art/product decision after the refresh-invariance bug is removed, not a reason to keep refresh-dependent behavior.
